Mixed-signal IC / PROJECT NOTES

9-bit Differential SAR ADC

A fully custom-layout 9-bit differential SAR ADC in TSMC 65nm with bootstrapped sampling switches, achieving 50.5 dB SNDR and 59.5 dB SFDR, taped out as part of a 6-group top chip.

This project involved the full custom design, layout, and post-layout verification of a 9-bit differential SAR ADC in TSMC 65nm using Cadence Virtuoso. Every analog core block was hand-laid from scratch: the comparator, bootstrapped sampling switches, inverters, skewed buffers, and RS latch, with careful attention to matching, noise isolation, and parasitic symmetry.

Comparator & CDAC Layout

The dynamic two-stage comparator (Van Elzakker, JSSC 2010) was laid out using a common-centroid differential pair, with dummy devices back-annotated throughout to ensure every active device sees the same local environment. Guard rings isolate the sensitive analog nodes from digital switching noise. Monte Carlo mismatch simulation in ADE XL across different process corners confirmed a mean offset of <3.2 mV (FF) and <1.8 mV (SS), well within the ±0.5 LSB target.

The binary-weighted CDAC was generated with the help of SKILL scripts (LLM-assisted) for layout efficiency, surrounded by a full dummy capacitor ring. Iterative simulation-driven refinement brought all capacitor ratios to within <±0.2% of their ideal binary-weighted values, critical for achieving monotonic code transitions.

Bootstrapped Sampling Switches

Referencing Prof. Razavi's bootstrapped switch topology (UCLA), a custom bootstrapping network was designed to hold the gate-source voltage VGS of the top-plate sampling switches nearly constant across the full input swing, achieving a VGS variation of only ~25 mV. This dramatically linearizes the on-resistance RON, with the sampling switch network alone improving SFDR from 43 dB to 59 dB.

Digital Controller & Layout Methodology

The digital SAR controller was designed in VerilogA and taken through synthesis and place-and-route, with IO pin locations customized to minimize routing distance to the analog blocks while keeping the overall controller footprint compact. Across all layouts, odd metal layers were routed vertically and even layers horizontally, digital signal traces were physically separated from analog to suppress crosstalk, and wire lengths were matched wherever parasitic symmetry mattered, both for RC delay in digital paths and mismatch control in analog ones.

Post-Layout Verification

After full R+C+Cc parasitic extraction, the ADC was simulated in ADE L for SNDR and SFDR. With metal fillers and DCAP cells in place, the final verified performance is:

  • SNDR: 50.5 dB
  • SFDR: 59.5 dB
  • Power: 233.3 μW
  • Die Area: 399.2 × 341.5 μm

ADE L was also used to validate comparator propagation delay across corners, and ADE XL Monte Carlo runs covered both device mismatch and full PVT corner sweeps.

Top Chip Tape-Out

The design was subsequently merged with five other groups' SAR ADCs into a 6-ADC top-level tape-out chip. To support testability, a 6-to-1 output MUX (VerilogA) was designed to select the 10-bit output from any of the six ADCs, along with a 10-bit buffer array to restore signal strength along the long on-chip traces from ADC outputs to pads.

At the top level, the pad ring was modified to give each SAR ADC its own VDD domain while sharing a common VSS, and ESD protection was added to all exposed pads. Full DRC and LVS closure was achieved on the complete top chip — including density checks, antenna rule checks, and seal ring insertion to prevent fabrication edge defects.
