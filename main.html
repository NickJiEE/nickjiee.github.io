View My Work

From architecture to implementation.

I'm interested in how digital architectures become efficient hardware. My work spans RTL design and verification, synthesis, and physical design, with a focus on AI/ML accelerators and the tradeoffs between power, performance, and area. As a graduate researcher in UC San Diego's VVIP Lab, I translate architecture concepts into RTL and evaluate them through hardware implementation.

FAQ

What are your main areas of interest? ▼
My main interests are digital IC design, physical design, and AI/ML accelerator architectures. I enjoy connecting RTL and microarchitecture decisions to their impact on timing, power, and area after synthesis and place-and-route.
What is your experience with the RTL-to-GDS flow? ▼
For my machine learning accelerator project in TSMC 65 nm, I worked on Verilog RTL, synthesis with Synopsys Design Compiler, and physical implementation with Cadence Innovus. I also developed and debugged gate-level simulations with SDF back-annotation in Xcelium, checking hardware results against software-computed values.
What have you worked on in physical design? ▼
Beyond accelerator place-and-route, I've explored multi-bit flip-flop clustering and clock tree synthesis in OpenROAD. I built a Python clustering optimizer and reusable Tcl flows to compare placement and clock-tree strategies. In one CTS experiment, adjusting sink clustering reduced post-CTS max-fanout violations from 194 to 5 while maintaining timing closure.
What are you researching now? ▼
I'm a graduate researcher in the VVIP Lab at UC San Diego, advised by Professor Mingu Kang and working with Ph.D. student Yun-Chia Yu. My research focuses on AI/ML accelerator architecture and VLSI implementation, translating ideas from research literature into RTL, verifying their behavior, and carrying designs through synthesis and physical implementation for evaluation.
Which project best reflects your digital design interests? ▼
My dual-core machine learning accelerator brings together the parts of digital design I enjoy most: systolic-array computation, pipelined datapaths, asynchronous communication between independently clocked cores, and physical implementation. Optimizing the design showed me how architectural and implementation choices affect power, performance, and area. You can explore it on my Projects page.
What are your future goals? ▼
I want to deepen my skills and knowledge in digital IC and physical design, especially timing analysis, clock tree synthesis, and power, performance, and area optimization. I'm excited to contribute to efficient compute hardware and develop a stronger understanding of the complete path from architecture to silicon.
